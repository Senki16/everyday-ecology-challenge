Step by step

The process

Six steps spread over roughly six weeks. Most of that time is waiting — the hands-on work adds up to about two hours in total. Click any photo to enlarge it.

Step 01

Soak the rice

We combined two parts water to one part rice and left it to soak for three days. This draws the starches out of the grain and gives the bacteria something to feed on.

3 days

Step 02

Mix the rice water with milk

The rice water was collected into a clean container, and one spoonful of rice water was mixed with ten spoonfuls of milk. The milk is what the lactic acid bacteria multiply in.

Same day

Step 03

Let the mixture separate

The mixture rested for 15 days, until the casein separated from the whey. The whey — the clear yellowish liquid — is the part that carries the microorganisms forward.

15 days

Step 04

Combine whey, molasses and water

The whey was mixed with molasses and water in a proportion of 50 ml of whey and 50 ml of molasses per litre of water. This mixture was then left for one week.

1 week

Step 05

Fold the liquid into the rice husk

The molasses mixture was then combined with 2 kg of rice husk and mixed by hand until evenly damp. Any biodegradable material works here — shredded paper is a perfectly good substitute.

~30 minutes

Step 06

Ferment, and the bokashi is ready

After another 15 days sealed away, the bokashi was ready — dark, crumbly and faintly sweet-smelling. From here on it becomes the starter that gets sprinkled over the kitchen waste.

15 days
